How the week plays out

The shape of the story and the ways it can end. For how each part works underneath, see the mechanics guide. The world editor has every script.

Spoilers. This guide says what is really going on in the house. If you want to solve the mystery yourself, play first.

The premise

You and four old friends spend a week clearing your late great-aunt Hester's house before it is sold. On the first evening you force the attic door and find the Glass: a tall mirror that grants one wish a day to each person who speaks to it alone, in their own four-hour watch.

That night the group agrees one rule: nobody wishes on anyone else in the house. Each of you only changes yourself.

The secret is that one friend's first wish bound the Glass to that rule for everyone except themself. From then on, changes nobody owns up to keep landing on the rest of you. The group calls them echoes, spill from their own wishes. They're not.

Two ways to play

Detective · the default

Find who is doing it

You are one of the friends who keeps waking up changed. Use your own wish each day as you like, watch the others, and work out who broke the pact before the seventh midnight.

Cheater · a code from the endings

Be the one doing it

You bound the Glass. Each day you can wish on anyone, while the friends try to work out why. Keep your secret to the seventh midnight, or get caught.

How the case comes together

The answer can't be guessed early. It is built from facts, each one learnable only once the story has shown its evidence. The Case File records the facts you've learned.

  • The old ways
    Hester's ledger and letters: how the Glass works, and that it once let her wish on anyone.
  • The fizzle
    A wish on someone else does nothing now. One friend admits it around day 3, or you try it yourself.
  • The binding
    Put those two together: someone changed the Glass's rules, most likely with a wish.
  • The misfit
    The "echoes" don't fit the theory. Some land with no wish just before, or on several people at once.
  • The cheat
    Whoever bound the Glass left themself free, and is doing this on purpose.
  • Then two of three
    The changes carry one friend's taste. That friend's claimed wishes never show. The changes always come within hours of their watch.
  • The culprit
    Accuse them with that evidence, and they break and admit it.

One innocent friend shares the culprit's taste, and another's wishes are as quiet as the culprit's for the first days. So no single clue is enough; you need two. A wrong accusation makes the culprit bolder. Two wrong accusations split the group.

After the week

Every ending except a broken mirror leads into an open sandbox, picking up from where your story stopped. The ending decides which of three arrangements you get:

The Glass is yours

Wish on anyone, once a day. The changes are still logged.

A friend keeps it

They make one wish a day, from their own tastes, on you or on the others.

Nobody uses it

By agreement or because it's broken. The changes stay, and the week goes on.

The mystery is over by then. Everyone is open about what they wished, and the case tools stop running, so turns cost less.
